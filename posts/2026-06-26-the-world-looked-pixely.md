Two things were wrong with the Nod opening, and they turned out to be unrelated problems that
happened to look like one problem.

**A.** The world looked *pixel'y* — uneven, chunky pixels on the shack and on the midground and
background outlines.

**B.** The midground and background traced the foreground silhouette exactly, with no shape of
their own. Stacked cutouts of the same mountain.

I deliberately did not touch colour or haze. The warm amber hazey look stays.

## Change 1 — giving MG/BG their own silhouette

`Assets/Sprites/RegionGenerationConfig.asset` **CHANGED**

| field | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `mgDilateNoiseTiles` | `0.99` | `2.5` |
| `bgDilateNoiseTiles` | `0.58` | `3` |
| `dilateNoiseWavelengthTiles` | `45.41` | `18` |

The midground and background are generated by dilating the foreground mask outward — midground at
radius 6, background at radius 10. There's a per-column Perlin "wander" applied to that radius, and
it was set *sub-tile*. Under a tile of wander, the two layers can never be anything but a near-constant
offset of the foreground. Hence stacked cutouts.

Effective radius is `radius ± noiseTiles`, and the two layers use decorrelated noise (salts 137 and
911). At 2.5 and 3.0, the combined wander of 5.5 exceeds the 4-tile gap between the radii — so the
midground can now locally rise **above** the background. A midground hill peeking over a background
ridge. Both diverge from the foreground outline instead of shadowing it.

Wavelength 18 instead of 45 makes the undulation read as hills rather than a slow constant drift.

Still tunable. Too lumpy → lower the noise values toward 1.5 / 2.0. Still too foreground-like → raise
them. Needs a re-bake either way.

## Change 2 — softening the silhouette edges

`Assets/BakeProfile.asset` **CHANGED**

| field | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `midgroundEdgeFeatherPixels` | `3` | `6` |
| `backgroundEdgeFeatherPixels` | `5` | `9` |

The back-layer silhouette is a hard alpha edge sitting on a 10px-per-tile grid. A 3px feather isn't
enough to hide that, so the outline stayed visibly chunky. Widening it dissolves the edge into the
haze, which also suits the look I want anyway.

## Change 3 — pixel-snap zoom

`Assets/Scripts/CameraFollow.cs` **CHANGED**

This is the one that actually fixed complaint A, and the cause is slightly infuriating.

The world bakes at 100 art-pixels per unit and is point-filtered. The resting camera sits at
orthographic size 2.1, which at 1080p magnifies the art by a **fractional** ~2.6x. With no
pixel-perfect snapping, some texels land across 2 screen pixels and others across 3. That
inconsistency *is* the chunky look — it was never the art.

So the camera now rounds its resolved orthographic size so that screen-pixels-per-art-pixel is a
whole number. At 1080p it lands on 3x, which happily also matches the player's 1/3-resolution render
cadence. Snapping is skipped during cinematics so the cliff-slide zoom stays smooth.

There is a real tradeoff here, and it's worth being honest about it:

> Snapping shifts the resting framing. Orthographic 2.1 becomes 1.8 at 1080p — about 14% closer than
> before. It can also step by one pixel-level during combat zoom.

If that framing change turns out to be worse than the uneven pixels were, `pixelSnapZoom` is a
serialized bool and turning it off reverts the whole thing instantly.

## Not changed, but recommended

On `Main Camera → BackgroundDepthComposite`, setting `textureFilter` from Point to **Bilinear** is
the most direct softener for individual pixels in the MG/BG outline, and it leaves the foreground and
player crisp. I left it alone because it's scene-serialized and I didn't want to edit the open scene
out from under myself — but it's a one-click inspector change if the back layers still read as harsh.
