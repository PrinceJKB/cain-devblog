I've been circling The Wake for a while without being able to say what it *was*. Regions that don't
know what they're about turn into a pile of features. So: locked.

**The Wake is the region about reading the forest.** **LOCKED 2026-08-02**

Threats and rewards are both camouflaged, and everything the region teaches is a rule for telling
them apart. A new player walks past three secrets and then gets ambushed by what they were confident
was a tree. A veteran reads the same screen and knows where the loot is buried and which silhouette
is about to stand up.

That's the region's *verb identity* — the thing it makes you do that no other region does. Harthen
Village's identity is "you are an outsider in someone's territory." The Wake's is "you cannot trust
what you're looking at until you've learned how to look."

## Why the caves belong here

The surface and the caves are the same skill, inverted.

On the **surface**, you read the forest to find things. It's sight-led. In the **caves**, the forest
reads *you* — the blind wretches down there hunt by sound. It's sound-led.

This is the bit I'm most pleased with, because it means the cave section isn't a separate minigame
bolted onto the region. It's the region's core skill with the senses swapped, which is precisely why
it belongs in The Wake and nowhere else.

## The vocabulary of tells

The mastery curve here is a vocabulary. Each tell has to be learnable purely by observation, never
explained by UI, and — this is the non-negotiable one — **always true.**

A tell that lies even once is worthless forever. The moment a player learns that fire *usually*
hides something, the whole system collapses into guesswork, and guesswork isn't mastery.

Locked so far:

- **Fire and light sources hide things behind them.** Anything lit conceals what's past it.
- **Passive wretches cluster near buried valuables.** A sitting, non-hostile wretch is a loot marker
  *and* a decision, because waking it is expensive.
- **Certain roots retract from the Abel-dagger.**

On the table, not yet agreed:

- **Camouflaged wretches creak before they move.** The audio tell arrives before the visual one, which
  rewards players who actually listen. The silhouette is subtly wrong if you know what to look for —
  breathing, a bark rhythm that isn't quite right, an exo-suit port catching the light.
- **Less-overgrown ruins mean recent disturbance.** Where the forest hasn't reclaimed something,
  something has been keeping it clear. Occupied.
- **Marrow-animals leave sign.** Stripped bark, tracks, disturbed root mats. Follow the sign, find
  the animal.
- **Spore patches attract wretches** — a hazard you can weaponise as a lure.
- **Wet ground carries sound.** A cave rule, foreshadowed on the surface.

## The filter

Every Wake feature now gets judged against one question:

> Does this give the player something to read, or something to learn to read?

If it does neither, it belongs in a different region. That's already killed two ideas I liked, which
is roughly how I know the filter is working.

## One thing still broken

:::spoiler An unresolved problem with a lore drop
The Vinessa Korell log in The Wake was gated behind a Mark attack. Mark isn't an attack any more —
it's the one-per-run revive now — so the gate is attached to a mechanic that no longer exists, and
the log is currently unreachable by design.

It needs a replacement dagger-specific interaction. The retracting roots are the obvious candidate to
build on, since they're already locked and already dagger-specific. That would make the log a reward
for having learned a tell, which is more in keeping with the region than a combat gate ever was.
:::
